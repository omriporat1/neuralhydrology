## What was done

- **Hyperparameter search closed (Oct 13).** 36 configurations on 400 screening basins. No configuration wins on everything. The one with the best median NSE (P18, 0.45) is weak on flood peaks; P19 has the best KGE and is the most robust across checks. We stopped the search there: more search on NSE alone was unlikely to help floods.
- **Longer input windows help (Oct 14-21).** Tested 72 to 336 hours, 3 random seeds each. On all 2,307 development basins, median NSE rose from about 0.43 (120 h) to 0.50 (240 h) and 0.53 (336 h). About 70% of basins improved from 120 h to 336 h. For floods specifically, 120 h is worse than the longer windows, but 240 h and 336 h are not clearly different.
- **Decision (Oct 22): stop exploring and fix a baseline.** All of this is judged on 2024 only, the same year used for all the tuning. And the 336 h models were still improving when training stopped. So more length testing on 2024 would not settle much. A better evaluation design comes first; the length question can return inside it. [edit: your reasoning, if different]
- **Water-year rotation designed and checked for feasibility (Oct 22).** Data for water years 2021-2025 is all in place. Water year 2026 needs new downloads (Jan-Sep 2026). Cost: about 80 GPU-hours for 5 cycles with one seed, plus about 100 for choosing checkpoints on all 2,307 basins. That is roughly one to two days of wall time on Moriah.

## Already decided (Omri, Nov 11), for information

- **The rotation is adopted**, including the change to protected data: Jan-Sep 2025 becomes development (water year 2025), and water year 2026 becomes the protected test year.
- **Checkpoints are chosen on all 2,307 development basins** (not the 400 screening subset), since each cycle has a whole year reserved for it. This adds about 20 GPU-hours per cycle.
- **Water year 2026 data is prepared in parallel.** Download and package Jan-Sep 2026 while the rotation runs, then score all models on that same year. Comparing models on the same year and the same events shows how much skill depends on which years were used for training, checkpoint choice and assessment.
- Stage 2 (adapting the model to drier basins) is not on the agenda yet.

## To discuss

1. **A second, flood-oriented training objective in the same rotation.** So far training and selection both optimise median NSE, but our interest is flash floods. Options:
   - **(a) Flood-aware checkpoint choice, no new training.** Pick the epoch by a flood metric (for example NSE on hours above the observed Q98) on the checkpoint year. It is almost free, because it reuses the same runs.
   - **(b) Weighted NSE loss.** Each hour weighted by its observed flow relative to the basin's high flows. It is a small change with one parameter, and it still trains on all hours.
   - **(c) Asymmetric loss.** Extra penalty for under-predicting high flows. It targets our common failure, but needs a guard against over-prediction and false alarms.
   - **(d) Probabilistic output.** NeuralHydrology can predict a distribution (CMAL head); flood use then reads an upper quantile. This addresses a likely cause of under-predicted peaks: a squared-error model predicts the average, and peaks are the most uncertain hours.
   - NSE on Q98+ hours alone as the *loss* is risky: it ignores most hours, so false alarms go unpenalised.
   - Suggested: (a) for both arms from the start; one training arm, (b) or (d), launched after the baseline's first cycle passes. All arms judged on the same, fixed set of overall and flood metrics.
2. **Training budget (12 epochs of up to 50k updates).** In the search, P19's best epoch was 7 of 12, so at 120 h the cap does not seem to bind. The 336 h runs did hit it. Suggested: keep the cap for the 120 h baseline so it stays comparable with the search, flag any cycle whose best epoch is 11 or 12, and revisit for longer windows or a new loss.
3. **Which window length goes into the rotation?** 240 h and 336 h scored better than 120 h on 2024.
   - Options: (a) 120 h only, as the baseline; (b) 120 h and 336 h side by side, about twice the compute.
   - Suggested: (a) first. [edit]
